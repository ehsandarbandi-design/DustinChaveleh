If you're house hunting in San Francisco with school-age kids, there's a piece of local logic that you should know:

**San Francisco is both a city and a county, and it's served by a single unified school district:** [**SFUSD**](https://www.sfusd.edu/)**.** There's no Noe Valley school district. No Marina school district. No invisible line where "the good schools" end and everything else begins, tied to your street address.

![](/images/blog/schools-in-san-francisco/1.webp)

## The Standard Playbook Doesn't Apply Here

In most markets, school district lines are one of the biggest drivers of home value. Two houses a block apart, split by a district boundary, can carry very different price tags because one feeds a stronger district and the other doesn't. Buyers build their entire search around it — you find the district first, then the house.

That plays two ways:

-   **The upside:** you're not locked out of a neighborhood you love just because it's not "zoned" for the school you want. You get to choose where to live based on commute, walkability, housing stock, and lifestyle — not school zoning alone.
    
-   **The catch:** getting into a specific school means navigating an application and preference process through SFUSD, not just buying the right address.
    

## Moving in the Summer?

In most suburban markets, families move over the summer and are automatically zoned into their new school by fall. The whole system is built around that rhythm — move, unpack, start school, done.

San Francisco doesn't run on that schedule. Because enrollment isn't tied to your address, "move in August, start fresh in September" doesn't map onto SF the way people expect. Summertime in San Francisco is actually a slower time for market activity, and picks back in in the Spring/Fall.

### What This Means for Your Search

If schools are part of your decision, the move is to decouple the two questions:

1.  **Choose your neighborhood** based on what's actually neighborhood-specific — commute, walkability, housing stock (condo vs. TIC vs. single-family), lifestyle fit.
    
2.  **Handle school enrollment separately**, on its own timeline, directly through SFUSD — rather than assuming your address locks in an outcome.
    

Once you understand SF operates as one unified district, you stop burning time chasing "zoned schools" that don't exist the way you'd expect — and you can focus your house search on what actually does vary block to block in this city.
