## The Baseline: Who Pays What in California?

In a standard California real estate transaction, the **seller customarily pays the documentary transfer tax**. Across most of the state, this is a relatively minor, flat expense. The baseline California county rate is **$1.10 per $1,000** of property value.

For example, on a **$1,000,000 deal** in a standard California market, the transfer tax would be a flat **$1,100**.

However, individual municipalities are permitted to layer their own city-level transfer taxes on top of the county baseline. As outlined in the breakdown above, local closing custom in San Francisco mandates that the seller absorbs the entirety of our city's progressive real estate transfer tax liability.

## The San Francisco Reality: A Progressive Tax Scale

San Francisco does not use a flat rate. Instead, the city implements a highly progressive, tiered system where the tax rate applies to the **entire transaction amount**, not just the portion within that specific bracket.

Below is the current tax breakdown, alongside the approximate percentage of total city home sales trading within each respective band to illustrate where the market concentrates:

$250k to $1M: 0.68% ($3.40 per $500) ~18% of SF Sales

$1M to $5M: 0.75% ($3.75 per $500) ~74% of SF Sales

$5M to $10M: 2.25% ($11.25 per $500) ~6% of SF Sales

$10M to $25M: 5.50% ($27.50 per $500) ~1.5% of SF Sales

$25M and above: 6.00% ($30.00 per $500) < 0.5% of SF Sales

Because the rate shifts for the whole sale price, a modest shift in your final contract value can yield an exponential shift in what you owe the city.

## The $5 Million "Dead Zone"

The most dramatic friction point in our local market occurs right at the **$5,000,000 threshold**. When a transaction hits $5 Million, the transfer tax rate doesn't just increase slightly—it **triples** from 0.75% to 2.25%.

Because this jump applies to the absolute gross sale price, it creates a mathematical "cliff" that heavily impacts listing and bidding strategies. By accepting a contract that is exactly $1,000 higher, a seller takes home **$74,007.50 less** in net profit.

To bypass this financial penalty and pocket the same net proceeds as a $4.99M sale, a seller has to push the final contract price all the way to **$5,077,000**. This dynamic creates a literal "ghost town" of transaction data between $5.0M and $5.07M, as agents and sellers purposefully anchor properties right below the cliff line to ensure tax efficiency.

### Frequently Asked Questions

#### How to Find Your California Transfer Tax Rate

Check your county recorder's or assessor's office website for the current documentary transfer tax rate. For city-level taxes, search your city's municipal code or contact your escrow officer. Many California title and escrow companies publish local rate guides. Your escrow officer will calculate the exact amount and confirm both county and city charges before closing.

#### Can you adjust the property's cost basis to reduce taxes?

Yes. While documentary transfer taxes are not deductible on your federal or California state income tax returns, they can be added to your property's cost basis. This adjustment increases your baseline investment amount on paper, which effectively lowers your taxable capital gains exposure when computing your net earnings from the sale.

#### Are there legal ways to avoid the San Francisco transfer tax cliff?

Property transactions under cash structures, LLC transfer assessments, or short sales remain fully subject to transfer tax based on gross market values. However, verified legal exemptions exist under the California Revenue and Taxation Code. Common transfer tax exemptions include moving property into a revocable living trust, interspousal transfers resulting from a divorce settlement, and standard property inheritance transfers through probate court orders.
